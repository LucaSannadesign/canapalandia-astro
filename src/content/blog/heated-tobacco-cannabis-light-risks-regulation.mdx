Two heated-tobacco sticks, followed by measurable changes in blood-vessel function and blood-cell DNA damage markers. That is what researchers reported in an October 2026 study. Heated tobacco nevertheless remains a legally marketed product, while low-THC hemp flowers face sweeping restrictions in Italy.

Does this establish a double standard? **Not by itself.** But it raises a reasonable public-health question: how should regulators distinguish between controlling the risks of a legal product and prohibiting entire product categories?

> **Key takeaway:** the study documented immediate biological effects of heated tobacco, not permanent disease after two sticks. It did not compare tobacco with cannabis or show that smoking cannabis is safe. The central issue here is the **consistency and proportionality of regulation**.

## What happened after two heated-tobacco sticks?

On 27 October 2026, the *European Journal of Preventive Cardiology* published [research led by Lea Strohm at University Medical Center Mainz](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwag524).

The team examined **30 adult smokers** before and immediately after they used **two consecutive heated-tobacco sticks** under supervision with an IQOS device. Some measurements, including blood pressure and venous blood gases, were available for **24 participants**.

The researchers reported:

- **Reduced flow-mediated dilation** of the brachial artery, suggesting acute endothelial dysfunction.
- **Higher systolic blood pressure**, while diastolic pressure and heart rate did not change significantly in the study.
- **Changes in venous oxygenation and metabolic measurements** after exposure.
- **Increased DNA damage markers in blood cells**, measured with a comet assay.

These observations do not amount to a diagnosis of disease. They do show that heated-tobacco aerosol is **not biologically inert**, even though the product does not burn tobacco in the same way as a conventional cigarette.

The Italian newspaper [*Il Fatto Quotidiano* reported on the study on 11 November](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/11/11/bastano-due-stick-di-tabacco-riscaldato-per-provocare-alterazioni-ai-vasi-sanguigni-e-al-dna-il-nuovo-studio-che-mette-in-discussione-la-sicurezza-delle-alternative-alle-sigarette/8559318/).

### What does "DNA damage" mean here?

The comet assay detects signs of damage to genetic material in the cells examined. It does **not** prove that two sticks cause permanent mutations, cancer, or future illness.

The study was relatively small, assessed only immediate effects and used a pre-post design without a separate sham-exposure control group. It could not isolate nicotine's contribution from that of other aerosol constituents, and it did not compare heated tobacco directly with cannabis or even with conventional cigarettes.

The scientifically sound conclusion is specific: **one supervised heated-tobacco session was associated with acute vascular dysfunction and cellular damage indicators under the conditions studied**.

## Legal tobacco is not harmless tobacco

The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tobacco) estimates that tobacco use causes **more than seven million deaths worldwide each year**, including deaths associated with second-hand smoke. That figure covers tobacco overall; it is not a heated-tobacco-specific mortality estimate.

Tobacco is legal in Italy, but its market is regulated through age restrictions, taxes, health warnings, advertising rules and smoke-free protections. Its legal status does not imply that it is safe.

Lawmakers have chosen **regulated access to a known harmful product**, rather than a complete ban on its sale. Tobacco and cannabis have different political and regulatory histories, but history alone cannot replace an evidence-based explanation for present-day restrictions.

## Italy's restrictions on low-THC hemp flowers

**Article 18 of Decree-Law 48/2025**, subsequently converted into **Law 80/2025**, amended Italy's hemp framework under Law 242/2016.

It introduced extensive restrictions concerning hemp flowers, including trade, distribution, transport and retail sale, alongside sanctions referring to Italy's narcotics legislation. The framework contains a specific exception for processing flowers for agricultural seed production. Restrictions also cover certain products containing or consisting of those flowers and derivatives.

This must **not** be misrepresented as a blanket ban on the entire hemp plant or on every product containing CBD. The legal status of an item depends on the type of product, its source and composition, its intended use and other applicable sectoral rules.

## Why proportionality matters

Comparing the two substances does not, in itself, make Italy's restrictions unlawful. It does, however, support asking whether measures are **suitable, necessary and proportionate** to the public-health risks they aim to prevent.

The question is already before Italy's Constitutional Court. A [referral from the Court of Brescia, registered as Order 135/2026](https://www.cortecostituzionale.it/scheda-ordinanza/2026/135), raises issues including proportionality and the treatment of hemp-flower products without psychoactive or intoxicating effect in the specific case. A **public hearing is scheduled for 9 February 2027**.

That is a future hearing, **not a ruling that repeals the existing law**.

A separate case is before the Court of Justice of the European Union following [Italy's Council of State Order 6202/2026](https://www.giustizia-amministrativa.it/it/web/guest/-/105486-2039). This referral concerns restrictions on **oral preparations containing CBD derived from cannabis extracts**. It is not a general decision about all hemp flowers.

The cases differ, but both show why precise product definitions and properly justified restrictions matter.

## Cannabis also carries health risks

A serious argument for consistent regulation cannot pretend cannabis is harmless.

THC can impair memory, attention, coordination and driving performance. Some patterns of use are associated with dependence and elevated risks for adolescents and vulnerable people.

[Smoking cannabis can harm lung tissue](https://www.cdc.gov/cannabis/health-effects/lung-health.html), and its smoke contains many irritants and toxic substances also found in tobacco smoke. [Cardiovascular concerns](https://www.cdc.gov/cannabis/health-effects/heart-health.html) have also been raised, although important uncertainties remain.

Low-THC hemp flowers, isolated CBD, THC-rich products and smoked cannabis should not be treated as interchangeable. Nor does a heated-tobacco study offer any basis for claiming that cannabis is safer than tobacco in all situations.

Effective policy requires product-specific assessments of **health risks, dependence potential, exposure, intended use and population vulnerability**.

## Public-health evidence should guide the rules

Public authorities have a legitimate duty to protect health and children. The goal is not to demand a total tobacco ban simply because hemp flowers are restricted, or to call for unrestricted sales of every cannabis-derived product.

The point is to apply **transparent scientific criteria and proportionate safeguards** to different products.

A legal substance may be dangerous. A prohibited product is not necessarily more dangerous simply because it is prohibited. Explaining the distinction requires evidence, not slogans.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did two heated-tobacco sticks cause permanent DNA damage?

The study found indicators of DNA damage in blood cells after one session. It did not test for permanent mutations or long-term disease outcomes.

### Is cannabis safer than tobacco?

This study cannot answer that. Risks depend on specific products, doses, routes of administration and patterns of use. Smoking cannabis is not risk-free.

### Have Italian courts legalised low-THC hemp flowers?

No. The pending proceedings discussed here have not, by themselves, repealed Italy's current rules.
