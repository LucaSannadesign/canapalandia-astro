<p>While Italy reclassifies CBD as a narcotic and burdens patients with new restrictions, <strong>Slovenia and Albania take a progressive step forward</strong> by legalizing medical cannabis.</p>
<p>Two very different countries, yet both have recognized the therapeutic potential of cannabis and decided to act. And Italy? Still stuck in fear, ideology, and political inertia.</p>
<h2>📍 Slovenia: A New Law for Medical Cannabis</h2>
<p>The Slovenian Parliament has approved legislation that <strong>legalizes medical cannabis</strong>, making it available through the national healthcare system.</p>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.aduc.it/notizia/cannabis+terapeutica+approvata+legge+all_141079.php" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Aduc</a>, the new law allows:</p>
<ul>
<li>Doctors to prescribe cannabis-based treatments</li>
<li>Authorized and monitored domestic cultivation</li>
<li>Distribution through official medical channels</li>
</ul>
<p>A clear, patient-focused approach that aims to remove stigma and offer real therapeutic options.</p>
<h2>📍 Albania: Legal Cannabis for Production and Export</h2>
<p>In 2023, Albania passed a law allowing the <strong>production, processing, and export</strong> of medical and industrial cannabis. The law took effect in 2024 and established a state agency to oversee licensing and quality control.</p>
<p>✔️ Regulated foreign investment<br />✔️ Controlled domestic production<br />✔️ Economic development for agricultural zones</p>
<p>Once seen as conservative on drug policy, Albania is now embracing cannabis as a therapeutic and economic resource.</p>
<h2>🇮🇹 Italy: Back to Repression</h2>
<p>Italy’s recent moves show the opposite trend:</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>“Decreto Sicurezza 2025”</strong> bans all sales of cannabis light flowers, leaves, and resin—even with < 0.2% THC</li>
<li>The <strong>Regional Administrative Court (TAR) of Lazio</strong> confirmed that oral CBD oil must be classified as a narcotic</li>
<li>Now even CBD requires a <strong>non-repeatable medical prescription</strong> from a physician</li>
</ul>
<p>This isn’t regulation. It’s prohibition dressed in bureaucracy.</p>
<h2>👥 What About Italian Patients?</h2>
<p>Here’s the reality for people who rely on medical cannabis in Italy:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Long waiting times</strong> for prescriptions</li>
<li><strong>Shortages</strong> in galenic pharmacies</li>
<li><strong>High costs</strong> for those without coverage</li>
<li><strong>Fear of being judged or criminalized</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>On paper, cannabis is allowed for therapeutic use. In practice, <strong>it&#8217;s a right denied or only granted to the privileged few</strong>.</p>
<h2>📌 Did you know?</h2>
<ul>
<li>CBD is sold freely in most EU countries. In Italy, a bottle of CBD oil now requires a special prescription</li>
<li>Italy imports medical cannabis from abroad—while criminalizing its own production</li>
<li>Medical cannabis is legal in theory, but still treated like a taboo</li>
</ul>
<h2>🌍 European Map: Who’s Moving Forward?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>🇩🇪 Germany:</strong> Legalization of recreational cannabis underway</li>
<li><strong>🇸🇮 Slovenia:</strong> New medical cannabis law passed</li>
<li><strong>🇦🇱 Albania:</strong> Legal for production and export under state control</li>
<li><strong>🇮🇹 Italy:</strong> Criminalization of CBD, regression in patient access</li>
</ul>
<p>Result? Italian patients are left behind—health-wise and in basic human dignity.</p>
<h2>🎯 Conclusion: Health Has No Borders—But Politics Builds Them</h2>
<p>Medical cannabis isn’t a trend. It’s a science-backed treatment that improves lives. Slovenia and Albania show it’s possible to move forward. Italy, on the other hand, is walking backward.</p>
<p>As long as fear governs policy, <strong>patients will suffer and society will stay in the dark</strong>.</p>
