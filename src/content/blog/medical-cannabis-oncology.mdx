<p><strong>Medical cannabis</strong> is gaining recognition as a valuable tool in cancer care, offering support in symptom management and, in some cases, showing potential as an <strong>anti-tumor agent</strong>. Despite decades of <strong>demonization</strong>, a growing body of scientific evidence suggests this plant deserves serious medical consideration.</p>

<h2>Recent Scientific Evidence</h2>

<p>A meta-analysis published in <em>Frontiers in Oncology</em> reviewed over 10,000 international studies. Seventy-five percent of these confirmed cannabis&#8217; therapeutic relevance in oncology, highlighting significant benefits for patients.</p>

<p><strong>Sources:</strong> <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fonc.2022.879633/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Frontiers in Oncology – Therapeutic Use of Cannabinoids in Cancer: A Systematic Review</a></p>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://nursetimes.org/cannabis-terapeutica-nuovo-studio-ne-conferma-gli-effetti-positivi-come-trattamento-anti-cancro" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NurseTimes – Medical cannabis: new study confirms its positive effects</a></p>

<h2>Proven Benefits of Medical Cannabis in Oncology</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Pain relief:</strong> cannabinoids act on CB1 and CB2 receptors, providing relief from chronic, refractory pain.</li>
  <li><strong>Nausea and vomiting:</strong> effective especially in patients undergoing aggressive chemotherapy.</li>
  <li><strong>Appetite stimulation:</strong> essential for patients with cancer-related cachexia.</li>
  <li><strong>Anti-inflammatory properties:</strong> inhibition of inflammatory mediators via CBD and specific terpenes.</li>
  <li><strong>Anti-tumor effects:</strong> in vitro and animal studies show cannabinoids reduce cell proliferation and metastasis.</li>
  <li><strong>Psychological support:</strong> improvement in sleep quality and reduction of anxiety and depression in oncology patients.</li>
</ul>

<h2>New Therapeutic Frontiers</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Combined therapies:</strong> clinical trials are investigating the synergy between cannabinoids and traditional chemotherapy.</li>
  <li><strong>Synthetic cannabinoid drugs:</strong> isolated molecules such as dronabinol and nabilone are already approved in various countries.</li>
  <li><strong>Entourage effect:</strong> the interaction between THC, CBD, and terpenes increases efficacy compared to isolated compounds.</li>
  <li><strong>Personalized oncology:</strong> some studies explore the tumor response based on genetic polymorphisms and endocannabinoid receptor expression.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Regulatory and Cultural Barriers</h2>

<p>Ironically, in Italy we&#8217;re still debating not only the medical use of cannabis, but also whether to ban <strong>CBD-rich cannabis light</strong>, which contains <strong>no psychoactive THC</strong>. Rather than promoting informed access and education, lawmakers seem more focused on <strong>banning what doesn&#8217;t even get you high</strong>, fueling irrational fears and blocking both therapeutic and economic potential.</p>

<p>Although evidence continues to accumulate, access to medical cannabis remains restricted due to outdated laws and cultural stigma. In Italy, cannabis has been legally regulated for medical use since 2013, yet patients still face limited availability, complex bureaucracy, and widespread misinformation.</p>

<p><strong>Science is here. Policy often isn&#8217;t.</strong> It’s time to close the gap between medical research and legislation—because every patient deserves access to evidence-based, safe, and effective care.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>Medical cannabis is proving its relevance in oncology, offering much more than just symptomatic relief. It&#8217;s time to move beyond ideological prohibition and recognize—based on data, not dogma—the therapeutic value of this plant.</p>

<p><strong>This is not a miracle cure. But it’s a possibility. And in medicine, possibilities are not to be burned.</strong></p>
