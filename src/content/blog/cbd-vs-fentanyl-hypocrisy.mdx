<p class="p1">In the <span class="s1"><b>United States</b></span>, fentanyl causes more deaths than war. In <span class="s1"><b>Italy</b></span>, authorities are pushing to ban <span class="s1"><b>low-THC cannabis</b></span>. These may seem like two unrelated situations, but they both tell the same story: a system that has lost all sense of proportion.</p>
<p class="p1">On one hand, we have a <span class="s1"><b>synthetic opioid 100 times stronger than morphine</b></span>, legally distributed for years as a painkiller and now spreading as a deadly substance on the streets. On the other, a plant that has <span class="s1"><b>never caused a single fatal overdose</b></span>, helping millions relax, sleep, or simply breathe a little easier—yet still seen as a threat.</p>
<p class="p1">A comparison between <span class="s1"><b>fentanyl</b></span> and <span class="s1"><b>CBD-rich cannabis</b></span> obviously makes no sense from a scientific or medical point of view. One is a high-risk synthetic drug, the other a natural plant with <span class="s1"><b>soothing and therapeutic potential</b></span>. But that’s exactly why this contrast matters: why do we continue to <span class="s1"><b>criminalize cannabis</b></span>, while far more dangerous compounds remain tolerated or even promoted?</p>
<hr />
<p class="p4"><b>💊 Fentanyl: The Legalized Monster</b><b></b></p>
<p class="p1">In the United States alone, <span class="s1"><b>fentanyl has caused over 100,000 overdose deaths</b></span> in the past three years—more than the total casualties of the Vietnam War. It’s said to be <span class="s1"><b>50 times stronger than heroin</b></span>, <span class="s1"><b>100 times more powerful than morphine</b></span>. A few milligrams can be lethal.</p>
<p class="p1">Despite these risks, fentanyl was prescribed freely and marketed aggressively by <span class="s1"><b>pharmaceutical companies</b></span>. What started as medicine became an epidemic. And yet, in the very same context, there’s now a call to <span class="s1"><b>ban low-THC cannabis</b></span>—a non-psychoactive flower.</p>
<hr />
<p class="p4"><b>🌱 Cannabis Under Attack: A Natural Enemy?</b><b></b></p>
<p class="p1">While the U.S. battles the fentanyl epidemic, Italy is turning its attention to <span class="s1"><b>CBD cannabis</b></span>, a regulated, legal product grown under strict agricultural laws, with <span class="s1"><b>THC levels below 0.2%</b></span>.</p>
<p class="p1">A flower.</p>
<p class="p1">A helpful remedy for many. A clean, legal industry for thousands of farmers. A gentle option for those who suffer from anxiety, insomnia, and chronic stress.</p>
<p class="p1">But this flower scares people.</p>
<p class="p1">Not because it kills—it doesn’t. It’s feared because <span class="s1"><b>it works without going through a pharmacy</b></span>.</p>
<p class="p1">Because it’s non-addictive.</p>
<p class="p1">Because it’s simple.</p>
<p class="p1">Because it’s natural.</p>
<hr />
<p class="p4"><b>💼 Two Standards, One Logic: Profit</b><b></b></p>
<p class="p1">The crackdown on <span class="s1"><b>low-THC cannabis</b></span> is not a public health issue. It’s a matter of <span class="s1"><b>power</b></span>, of <span class="s1"><b>narrative control</b></span>, and of <span class="s1"><b>industrial interests</b></span> that often speak the language of synthetic chemistry.</p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1"><b>Fentanyl</b></span> has been portrayed as salvation. Cannabis—even the non-psychoactive kind—is still treated as a threat. Yet one has caused a national health crisis. The other has not.</p>
<hr />

<p class="p1"><b>📣 Time to Change the Narrative</b><b></b></p>
<p class="p3">This is not just about substances. It’s about <span class="s1"><b>freedom</b></span>. <span class="s1"><b>Truth</b></span>. <span class="s1"><b>Justice</b></span>.</p>
<p class="p3"><span class="s1"><b>Cannabis</b></span> is not the enemy. The real threat is the <span class="s1"><b>ignorance</b></span>, <span class="s1"><b>fear</b></span>, and <span class="s1"><b>inconsistency</b></span> in how we talk about—and regulate—what we put into our bodies.</p>
<p class="p3">The <span class="s1"><b>plant that calms</b></span> does not kill. The <span class="s1"><b>pill that should heal</b></span>, too often does.</p>
<p class="p3">It’s time to shift perspectives. To stop fearing a plant. To start demanding <span class="s1"><b>clarity</b></span>, <span class="s1"><b>transparency</b></span>, and <span class="s1"><b>respect</b></span> for those who choose natural alternatives.</p>
<p class="p4"><b>Canapalandia will continue to explore these issues—with courage, with facts, and with heart.</b><b></b></p>
