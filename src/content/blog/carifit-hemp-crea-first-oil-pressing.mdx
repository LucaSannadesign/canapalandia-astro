On 14 January 2026, a small but telling event took place at a farm in Staffolo, in Italy’s Marche region: seeds from two new experimental hemp lines were pressed into oil for the first time. This was not the launch of a commercial product. It was a step in CARIFIT, a publicly supported research project connecting plant breeding, field trials and processing.

[CREA reported the event on 6 February 2026](https://carifit.crea.gov.it/2026/02/06/estratto-il-primo-olio-dalle-nuove-selezioni-crea-di-canapa/). The story matters precisely because it concerns an experiment with follow-up analyses still planned, not a finished industrial success story.

## A research project that begins with seeds

CARIFIT — short for CAnapa RIcerca Filiera ITaliana — is coordinated by CREA’s Research Centre for Cereal and Industrial Crops and supported by Italy’s Ministry of Agriculture, Food Sovereignty and Forests. Started in March 2023, its work spans plant genetics, analytical controls, harvesting technologies and first-stage processing.

The underlying question is practical: what has to work for a crop to become a reliable industrial feedstock? Identifying an end use is not enough. Seed quality, plant characteristics, methods of measurement, agricultural operations and the treatment of harvested materials all play a role.

In a [September 2025 update](https://carifit.crea.gov.it/2025/09/10/in-selezione-due-linee-di-canapa-industriale-allinterno-delle-attivita-del-wp1-task-1-2/), CREA explained that **Carifit1p** was being selected for a short growth cycle and seed production, whereas **Carifit2t** was being selected for later maturity, potentially suiting seed or biomass production. These are breeding targets, not proven commercial outcomes.

Among the project’s stated research goals are breeding new varieties, testing them in different Italian growing areas, and measuring agronomic and production characteristics. These are research objectives rather than evidence that a commercial supply chain has already been established.

## From Osimo to Staffolo: what actually happened

According to the project’s official report, researchers from CREA-CI, CREA-AN and CREA-IT attended the pressing at Azienda Agricola La Fonte in Staffolo, near Ancona, on 14 January 2026.

The seeds came from two lines named Carifit1p and Carifit2t, developed within the project and cultivated in 2025 at CREA-CI’s experimental farm in Osimo. The pressing yielded oil and flour intended for nutritional analyses planned as part of the research programme.

This connects three distinct stages: selection, experimental cultivation and an actual processing operation. By itself, it does not establish superior yields, commercial availability, continuous production or widespread adoption of the two lines.

## Why the oil and flour still need to be analysed

Hempseeds can yield components used for different purposes. In the CARIFIT case, CREA announced nutritional testing of both the extracted oil and flour, alongside comparisons of extraction technologies to examine how processing parameters affect oil quality.

Obtaining a sample is therefore a starting point, not the end of the research. Composition may depend on the starting material and processing conditions. Until the specific findings are available, it would be misleading to assign these lines precise nutritional benefits, health effects or commercial advantages.

Canapalandia already offers a separate practical guide to hempseed oil, its uses and storage. This story is about research milestones, not dietary advice.

## A supply chain does not end at harvest

CARIFIT’s work package on mechanisation and processing addresses seed harvesting for different purposes, retting and fibre separation, processing residues, hemp hurds and the economics of different harvesting routes. These are essential links between what a farmer grows and what a processor can actually use.

The Staffolo pressing is one documented step, not a full picture of the Italian hemp sector. It does not mean that machinery, logistics, processing-capacity or market challenges have been solved. It does show how experimental research can connect field production to a tangible processed material.

## Three questions worth following

First, repeatability: will the new lines perform consistently in further field trials and growing conditions? Second, quality: what will the measurements reveal about the oil and flour, and which methods will be used? Third, feasibility: which technical and economic steps would be needed to move from experiments to stable production?

These are editorial questions based on the programme’s goals, not findings that CREA has already established for the January event. The project site lists research activities and publications, but the initial oil pressing should not be mistaken for proof of profitability or superior product quality.

## A real story, not a fabricated interview

The value of this example is not a sweeping promise that hemp will transform Italian agriculture. It lies in a documented sequence: new lines, field trials, oil pressing, obtained samples and planned measurements. Research proceeds by testing the gap between an idea and evidence.

To tell meaningful stories about the hemp supply chain, it helps to look at precisely these bounded, verifiable steps — and to distinguish what has happened from what remains to be demonstrated.
