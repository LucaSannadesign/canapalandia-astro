Two books, a long history of personal experience, and a question that runs through cannabis culture: how can what we have learned become knowledge that can be shared? The journey of an independent author from the Netherlands, spanning public education, entrepreneurship and artificial intelligence.

A family history spanning three generations, first memories dating back to 1989, and an interest that, in Adelino Peterus Melo’s account, developed into around thirty years of observation and learning. The independent author from the Netherlands has brought this journey together in two books, in which knowledge of the plant meets personal experience, entrepreneurship and reflection on the future.

This journey is presented to international readers through two works: a volume dedicated to the plant and an autobiographical account. The decision to place a botanical and practical publication alongside a personal narrative suggests an interesting way of reading them: the history of knowledge is also the history of the people who contribute to building it.

In his conversation with Canapalandia, Melo retraces the origins of cbdbiooil, the international collaborations he says he developed, and the subsequent vision of Hemp-Tech NL, focused on materials and industrial applications of hemp. The thread connecting his story is the relationship between what he has learned and what he hopes to build from those experiences.

## Thirty years of observation, curiosity and learning

Throughout the twentieth century, cannabis was portrayed in contrasting ways. An agricultural plant, an object of prohibition, a subject of biomedical research and a symbol of cultural movements: its different identities helped determine how knowledge about it was produced and shared.

Melo’s account belongs to the realm of individual learning. In an excerpt from the preface, the author attaches particular importance to studying the work of Ed Rosenthal, to which he says he devoted about eight years. He describes Rosenthal as an important reference in his personal education.

Over the years, Melo says, his interest expanded from products and materials to new technologies. This development is also reflected in his transition from personal observation to writing, with the aim of making what he had learned over time accessible to readers.

The first of the two books, The Cannabis Testament — How a cannabis plant is cultivated, is presented as an illustrated volume devoted to cannabis, its biology and the author's observations. The English edition contains 46 chapters in six parts; the hardcover edition has ISBN 9789493576186.

The publicly available outline brings together botany, history, chemical composition, environmental relationships and personal perspectives. The table of contents also includes a reference section with a glossary, bibliography and questions for future research. Its structure relates the author’s experience to the botanical and scientific context of the plant.

A significant example is the chapter on the so-called entourage effect. Its title places the subject within the realms of fact, hypothesis and debate: a particularly important distinction, since possible interactions between cannabis compounds do not in themselves demonstrate greater clinical effectiveness for every preparation containing them. Distinguishing hypotheses from clinical findings remains central for those approaching these subjects.

The cultural value of a publication of this kind also lies in its ability to make otherwise specialist terminology accessible. Public education, however, is not indiscriminate simplification: it must also make the uncertainties, conditions and limits of available knowledge understandable.

With My Way in Cannabis, the focus of the narrative changes. Presented as a personal biography, the book draws attention to learning experiences, business initiatives and international collaborations. The five translated EPUB editions contain 42 illustrated chapters.

The English subtitle, From Plant to Medicine, From Idea to an International Business, describes the trajectory the author intends to recount. The autobiographical perspective allows readers to follow the relationship between his interest in the plant, innovation and entrepreneurship.

This second perspective makes the two publications complementary. While in the first volume the author seeks to organise his knowledge of the plant, in the second he reconstructs the human and professional context in which that knowledge took shape.

The business experiences described in the interview, including collaborations in the United Arab Emirates, are part of his autobiographical account. The vision for Hemp-Tech NL opens another chapter in his journey: industrial hemp as a raw material for materials and applications.

## The boundary between experience and scientific research

Cannabis culture has also developed through informal communities and the exchange of experiences. Recognising their historical role does not mean automatically attributing to every personal account the same reliability as controlled research.

An observation may suggest a hypothesis, but it does not rule out alternative explanations. Understanding whether a phenomenon can be generalised requires documenting the conditions in which it was observed, assessing the variables that may influence it and determining whether the results are reproducible. Even long experience may be affected by expectations, selection of results and the absence of appropriate comparisons.

The distinction becomes essential in healthcare. Research on cannabinoids spans different fields, from pharmacology to clinical trials. Evidence concerning a particular substance, formulation or therapeutic indication cannot be indiscriminately transferred to other preparations or situations.

Experience and science do not need to be placed in opposition. A carefully contextualised account can offer useful starting points; scientific investigation can confirm, qualify or refute them. A willingness to reconsider one's ideas is one of the conditions for credible public education.

## The Netherlands and the future of cannabis in Europe

The geographical context in which Melo's story unfolded is not incidental. Since the 1970s, the Netherlands has adopted a policy of conditional tolerance for sales in coffeeshops, distinguishing them from the regulation of other substances. For a long time, however, this approach coexisted with a supply chain that had not been legalised.

The Dutch government launched a controlled supply chain experiment specifically to study the possibility of regulating production, distribution and sales. The experimental phase began on 7 April 2025. In September 2026, materials relating to the first monitoring assessment were made public. This is an evaluation process, not liberalisation across the entire country.

The European landscape remains diverse. In its regulatory analyses updated in April 2026, the EUDA explains that there is no harmonised EU framework for illicit use and personal possession. National choices interact with international obligations and differing public health and safety priorities.

For Canapalandia, this diversity makes it important to have a discussion that goes beyond the choice between prohibition and legalisation. Information quality, prevention, the protection of minors, access to research and the evaluation of policy effects are concrete issues against which reforms can be assessed, without drawing conclusions that the data do not yet support.

## Writing and translating in the age of artificial intelligence

A distinctive feature of Melo's project is its international dimension. His website presents both works in Italian digital editions, alongside several other languages, and offers print editions in English and Dutch. Overcoming language barriers is part of the strategy for making the books more widely available.

Melo says he originally wrote in Dutch and used artificial intelligence to support writing and translation. He clarified that the translations had not undergone independent linguistic and technical review.

This is an important clarification. AI can help an independent author address the costs and difficulties of translation, but it does not guarantee the accuracy of scientific definitions, quotations, botanical terminology or legal references. A linguistically convincing text may contain errors that a non-specialist reader finds difficult to detect.

Transparency about the use of these tools is therefore a first step, not a substitute for verification. Qualified human review, especially for specialist content, can help preserve the original meaning and make communication more reliable across languages.

## The interview with Adelino Peterus Melo

We asked the author to describe his journey, the meaning of his publications and the prospects he sees for cannabis culture. The following answers are English translations of the interview originally supplied and approved in Italian.

**Canapalandia — How did your interest in cannabis begin around thirty years ago?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** I am the third generation in my family to have cultivated cannabis. My earliest memory dates back to 1989, when I was four years old. Around the age of twelve, I had my first plants. Since then, curiosity has developed into around thirty years of observation, experience and learning.

**Canapalandia — How have your knowledge and beliefs changed over the years?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** I have learned to observe the plant as a whole, compare experiences and avoid confusing a single observation with a universal conclusion. I also studied Ed Rosenthal's work for about eight years, comparing it with my own observations. Over time, my interest expanded to products, materials and new technologies.

**Canapalandia — What motivated you to write The Cannabis Testament?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** I wanted to gather and pass on the knowledge I had gained through years of experience, study and observation of the plant. The book represents my way of organising this body of knowledge and making it accessible to readers.

**Canapalandia — What do you hope to convey to readers through My Way in Cannabis?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** It is my personal story, with successes, mistakes, difficulties and new beginnings. The moral of my story is the future: what matters is not only what we have experienced, but above all what we are able to build from those experiences. For me, it is an evolution within an evolution.

**Canapalandia — What have been the most significant milestones in your entrepreneurial journey?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** The transition from observing the plant to studying resin, extracts and CBD; the founding of cbdbiooil; the development of products and international collaborations, including in the United Arab Emirates; and subsequently the vision for Hemp-Tech NL, focused on industrial hemp as a raw material for materials and industrial applications. These are distinct stages of a journey that continues to evolve.

**Canapalandia — How do personal experience and scientific research relate to one another in your publications?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** Practical experience can generate questions and hypotheses. Research, analysis and data help to test them. My personal observations are not a substitute for scientific evidence and should not be interpreted as medical claims.

**Canapalandia — How has cannabis culture in the Netherlands changed over the past thirty years?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** In my experience, much knowledge was once passed on in private settings, where anonymity and trust played an important role. Over time, I have seen technology, laboratories and research play a growing role. This is my personal perspective, not an exhaustive account of national history.

**Canapalandia — What prospects do you see for the future of cannabis in Europe?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** My vision is focused on safer and more controlled production, verifiable quality, sustainability and collaboration between research, experience and industry. I also see opportunities in industrial hemp, materials, genetics, artificial intelligence and robotics. These are prospects and objectives, not results that have already been demonstrated.

**Canapalandia — What role did artificial intelligence play in creating your works and their translations?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** I am the author of the works, and the experiences described are my own. I originally wrote in Dutch and used AI as a tool to support writing and translation. The translations have not undergone independent linguistic and technical review.

**Canapalandia — What lesson would you like to pass on to younger generations?**

**Adelino Peterus Melo:** Keep observing, studying, comparing and learning. Respect the plant, safety, the applicable rules and the value of knowledge. The past should serve to build a better future.

## A culture of knowledge, beyond individual certainties

Adelino Peterus Melo’s publishing project allows us to observe a broader phenomenon: the opportunity, now accessible to a growing number of authors, to transform personal experience into a publication intended for readers in different countries.

The cultural interest of a personal account also comes from its ability to relate individual memory, documented knowledge and questions that remain open. It is in this exchange that the dialogue between authors, readers and those who share knowledge with the public finds its meaning.

The challenge is to build a cannabis culture that avoids both stigmatisation and promotional oversimplification. A culture in which experiences can be told, claims verified and disagreements discussed openly. Melo's two publications offer an opportunity to reflect on this journey: from individual experience to the international circulation of ideas, and to the responsibilities of those who choose to turn them into a work intended for the public.
