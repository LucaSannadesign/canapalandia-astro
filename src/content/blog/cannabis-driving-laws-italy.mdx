Someone takes a CBD product, notices no particular effect and decides to drive. Does feeling normal settle the question? Not quite. Three separate issues need to be considered: whether a substance can affect attention and vehicle control; what the product actually contains, including any THC; and what Italian roadside testing and criminal law require. Confusing these questions leads to both exaggerated warnings and unwarranted reassurance.

## Key points

- CBD and THC are different compounds; THC is the main cannabinoid responsible for cannabis intoxication.

- Small controlled trials found no consistent worsening of driving after CBD-dominant cannabis or isolated oral CBD under their particular experimental conditions. These findings cannot be generalized to every person or product [3, 4].

- Prescription cannabidiol can cause drowsiness and sedation, potentially affecting driving [5].

- A “CBD” or “THC-free” label does not necessarily establish the absence of THC. Analyses have found labeling discrepancies in selected commercial samples, but these are not representative of every product [6, 7].

- Under Italy’s Article 187, use in the distant past is not in itself enough: there must be scientific grounds to infer that relevant residual effects could still impair driving. Proof of actual impairment in each individual case is not necessarily required [1].

- Do not drive if you feel drowsy, slowed down, dizzy or unable to concentrate.

## CBD and THC are not interchangeable

THC can affect attention, coordination and lane-keeping. A randomized trial published in JAMA in 2020 enrolled 26 healthy occasional cannabis users and compared THC-dominant cannabis, CBD-dominant cannabis, cannabis with similar THC and CBD content, and placebo. On-road tests conducted 40–100 minutes after vaporization showed significantly greater lane weaving with the two THC-containing conditions than with placebo. No significant difference was found for CBD-dominant cannabis. The study was small, only 22 participants completed all drives, and the authors could not exclude clinically meaningful impairment in the CBD-dominant condition [3].

This trial used vaporized cannabis in defined experimental doses, not the full range of CBD oils and preparations sold to consumers. It also found that CBD administered alongside THC did not reliably prevent THC-related driving impairment [3].

## What studies on isolated CBD show

A second randomized crossover study gave 17 healthy adults isolated oral CBD at 15, 300 or 1,500 mg, or placebo. Simulated driving was assessed at two intervals after administration. Several comparisons supported non-inferiority for CBD; others were inconclusive. No statistically significant effects on cognitive performance or subjective intoxication were observed. These are encouraging findings, but small participant numbers, acute dosing and a simulated setting limit generalization [4].

The defensible scientific conclusion is not “CBD makes driving safe.” It is that these studies did not find consistent deterioration under the conditions they tested. Evidence remains insufficient to cover chronic use, poorly characterized products, interactions with other medicines and every individual circumstance.

## Drowsiness and sedation: the warning that matters

Official European Medicines Agency information for Epidyolex, a prescription cannabidiol medicine, identifies somnolence and sedation and recommends avoiding driving until the patient can judge how treatment affects their abilities. A prescription medicine used at therapeutic doses is not interchangeable with every consumer CBD product. Nevertheless, its safety information shows why absence of intoxication does not always mean absence of effects on alertness [5].

Do not drive if you experience drowsiness, dizziness, slowed reactions or trouble concentrating. Feeling alert is not, by itself, an objective demonstration of unimpaired driving ability. People taking other medicines, especially those affecting the central nervous system, should seek individualized advice from a doctor or pharmacist.

## CBD products, THC traces and roadside tests

Cannabis testing generally targets THC or related markers rather than CBD itself, making the product’s actual composition important. A 2022 US study found THC in some CBD oils marketed as THC-free, while a separate 2024 analysis identified discrepancies between CBD labels and measured contents [6, 7]. These US samples do not describe the whole Italian market. A batch certificate can be informative, but it cannot guarantee a particular test result or legal outcome.

No single waiting period can guarantee safe driving or a negative test. Drug effects and analytical detection windows differ, and both vary with dose, substance, route of use, frequency and biological specimen. Statements such as “six hours is always enough” or “a test must be negative after two days” are not reliable safeguards.

## What Article 187 means now

Law No. 177/2024 removed the express reference to driving in a state of psycho-physical impairment from Article 187 of the Italian Road Code. In judgment No. 10/2026, filed on 29 January 2026, the Constitutional Court upheld the provision under a restrictive interpretation: use must be recent enough that a substance could still impair vehicle control and increase the danger to road safety [1].

The Court rejected the idea that use long before driving is sufficient. Normally, the offence must be supported by toxicological findings obtained close to the driving event and interpreted according to substance, quantity and biological matrix. Crucially, this does not necessarily require proof that the individual driver was actually impaired, as the earlier wording did: Article 187 remains a danger-based offence, provided the inference of potentially impairing residual effects is scientifically justified. The Court also referred to the joint Interior and Health Ministries circular of 11 April 2025, which highlighted the close temporal link between drug use and driving [1, 2].

Three questions must remain separate: an initial screening result can trigger further checks; confirmatory findings require scientific interpretation in context; and legal responsibility is assessed by the competent authorities under applicable procedural safeguards. The ruling does not grant immunity after a positive result, and a medical prescription is not an automatic exemption [1].

## How to read tests without shortcuts

- An oral-fluid screen may guide further testing, but does not by itself amount to a final determination of criminal responsibility.

- Blood testing may provide useful evidence about drug presence near the time of driving; concentrations must be interpreted alongside the substance and toxicological context.

- Urine can remain positive after some drug effects have ended, so a positive urine result alone cannot establish persisting impairment at the time of driving.

- Observed signs, driving behaviour, a collision, timing and chain of custody may all matter in the individual case.

## What to do before driving

- Do not drive if you feel drowsy, altered, dizzy or unable to concentrate.

- If you use a cannabinoid medicine, follow the prescription and patient leaflet; ask your doctor about your treatment and other medicines.

- Do not assume that “CBD,” “light,” “full spectrum” or “THC not detected” automatically means zero risk.

- Keep the packaging, batch details and product documentation, without treating them as an automatic defence.

- If a test leads to a formal allegation, obtain the documents and seek qualified medical or legal advice rather than improvising.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does CBD always impair driving?

No. Available controlled studies do not show uniform impairment after isolated CBD. They do not cover every dose, product, medicine combination or individual.

### Can CBD make you drowsy?

Yes, particularly in therapeutic settings and at medicinal doses. Drowsiness alone is enough reason not to drive.

### Can a full-spectrum product affect a roadside test?

It may contain THC. The practical risk depends on actual composition, dose, frequency of use and the testing procedure.

### Does a positive result automatically justify a conviction?

No. Judgment No. 10/2026 requires scientifically grounded reasons to infer that potentially impairing effects could remain at the time of driving. That is different from requiring visible symptoms or proof of actual individual impairment in every case [1].

### Does a medical prescription exempt a driver?

No. It explains the therapeutic context but does not authorize driving when treatment reduces the abilities needed for safety.

## Conclusion

Driving demands attention, coordination and rapid responses to unexpected events. Studies on CBD should be read in that context: CBD is not THC, but neither is it automatically harmless in every formulation or circumstance. Actual product composition, drowsiness and other medicines matter. Legally, Italy’s Constitutional Court rejected punishment based solely on distant past use, while maintaining responsibility where testing can support an inference of potentially dangerous residual drug effects. Scientific evidence, analytical detection and legal assessment are separate questions [1, 3–7].

This article is general information and does not replace medical assessment or legal advice on an individual case.
