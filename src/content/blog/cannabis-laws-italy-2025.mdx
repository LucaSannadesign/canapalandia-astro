## In short: is cannabis legal in Italy in 2026?

**Recreational cannabis is not legal in Italy.** Possession for personal use can fall under an administrative-sanctions regime rather than the criminal offence for trafficking, but that does **not** make possession lawful. Medical cannabis is available through regulated prescription channels. Industrial hemp has a separate legal framework. CBD and so-called “cannabis light” cannot be reduced to a simple rule such as “low THC = legal”: the legal treatment depends on the product, its origin, intended use and the rules that apply to that product category.

For tourists, the safest practical rule is simple: **do not assume that a product legally sold elsewhere in Europe is automatically lawful to possess, carry, consume or drive after using in Italy.**

This page was reviewed on **31 August 2026**. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Personal possession: decriminalized does not mean legal

Italy's main drugs law is Presidential Decree no. 309/1990. Under **Article 75**, illicitly importing, acquiring, receiving or possessing controlled substances for personal use can lead to **administrative sanctions**, including measures affecting a driving licence, firearms licence, passport or, for some non-EU visitors, a tourism residence permit.

That is different from saying recreational cannabis is legal. The legal classification also depends on the facts of the case, including whether the substance appears intended exclusively for personal use.

**Official source:** [DPR 309/1990, Article 75 — Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn%3Anir%3Astato%3A1990%3B309~art75%21vig=)

By contrast, conduct such as unauthorized sale, distribution, delivery or possession intended for non-personal use can fall under the criminal provisions of **Article 73**. Penalties depend on the precise offence and circumstances, so this guide deliberately avoids presenting one headline prison term as if it applied to every case.

**Official source:** [DPR 309/1990, Article 73 — Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn%3Anir%3Apresidente.repubblica%3Adecreto%3A1990%3B309~art73=)

## Industrial hemp: the 0.3% figure is not a universal “legal product” threshold

Industrial hemp cultivation is governed by a combination of Italian and EU rules. Law no. 242/2016 applies to permitted **Cannabis sativa L.** varieties in the EU common catalogue and regulates specified industrial/agricultural uses.

At EU Common Agricultural Policy level, Regulation (EU) 2021/2115 increased the maximum THC level relevant to hemp cultivated on land eligible for direct payments from **0.2% to 0.3%** from 1 January 2023. The older Regulation 1307/2013 cited by many legacy articles was repealed.

**EU source:** [European Commission notice on the 0.3% hemp threshold — EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2023/1365/oj/eng)

However, that **does not mean every finished product below 0.3% THC is automatically legal to sell or use in Italy**. Cultivation rules, food law, cosmetics law, medicines law and narcotics law are separate layers.

**Italian source:** [Law 242/2016 — Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn%3Anir%3Astato%3Alegge%3A2016%3B242=)

## Cannabis light and hemp inflorescences: what changed in 2025

Article 18 of Law no. 80/2025 changed the Italian hemp framework significantly. The amended Law 242/2016 excludes from its favourable industrial-hemp regime a broad range of activities involving **hemp inflorescences** and products containing them, including extracts, resins and oils derived from those inflorescences, except for the specific agricultural seed-production case set out in the law.

The amended Article 2 also expressly prohibits several activities involving hemp inflorescences and those derived products and refers back to the sanctions under the drugs legislation.

**Official sources:**

- [Law 80/2025, Article 18 — Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/2025/06/09/131/sg/pdf)
- [Current Law 242/2016, Article 2 — Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn%3Anir%3Astato%3Alegge%3A2016%3B242~art2=)

### Is the 2025 rule legally settled?

Not completely. In 2026, courts raised **constitutional questions** concerning Article 18. For example, a question published in February 2026 and another raised by the Tribunal of Trani in April 2026 challenge aspects of the new prohibition and have been sent to the Constitutional Court.

This means two things can be true at the same time:

1. **Article 18 is part of the current statutory framework**, so a practical guide cannot simply ignore it.
2. **Its constitutional compatibility is under judicial review**, so it is equally misleading to describe every contested issue as definitively settled.

**Official sources:**

- [Constitutional question published 25 February 2026 — Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/vediMenuHTML?atto.codiceRedazionale=26C00034&atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2026-02-25&tipoSerie=corte_costituzionale&tipoVigenza=originario)
- [Tribunal of Trani referral, 28 April 2026 — Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/corte_costituzionale/caricaArticolo?art.codiceRedazionale=26C00098&art.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2026-05-27&art.idArticolo=4&art.idSottoArticolo=0&art.progressivo=0&art.versione=1)

## What about CBD in Italy?

There is **no reliable one-line answer such as “CBD is legal in Italy.”** CBD may appear in very different product categories, and the applicable law depends on what the product is, how it is made, what it contains, how it is marketed and what it is intended for.

In particular:

- a rule relevant to industrial hemp cultivation does not automatically authorize a consumer product;
- food and supplement products must comply with the rules applicable to foods and novel foods;
- cosmetics must comply with cosmetics legislation and cannot be marketed with medicinal claims;
- medicinal products follow medicines and prescription rules;
- products constituted by or derived from hemp inflorescences are directly affected by the 2025 amendments described above.

For that reason, this guide does **not** label CBD oils, flowers, edibles or supplements as universally legal or illegal without examining the specific product and legal category.

## Medical cannabis in Italy

Medical cannabis is available in Italy through regulated medical channels. According to the Ministry of Health, doctors have been able to prescribe magistral preparations using cannabis-based active substances since 2006. The Ministry describes medical cannabis primarily as a **symptomatic/supportive treatment** used in specified circumstances, generally when conventional treatments are ineffective or poorly tolerated.

The Ministry lists uses including certain forms of chronic pain, spasticity-related pain, nausea and vomiting associated with cancer or HIV treatments, appetite stimulation in specified conditions, glaucoma and Tourette syndrome. This should not be read as a claim that cannabis cures those diseases.

Italian-produced FM1 and FM2 cannabis and authorized imports are distributed to pharmacies for magistral preparations on medical prescription.

**Official sources:**

- [Ministry of Health — Medical use of cannabis](https://www.salute.gov.it/new/it/tema/medicinali-stupefacenti-e-precursori-di-droghe/uso-medico-della-cannabis/)
- [Ministry of Health — Distribution of medical cannabis](https://www.salute.gov.it/new/it/tema/medicinali-stupefacenti-e-precursori-di-droghe/la-distribuzione-della-cannabis-ad-uso-medico/)

Tourists should not assume that a prescription issued abroad automatically permits them to obtain or carry a controlled cannabis medicine in Italy. The Ministry notes that EU cross-border prescription recognition has exceptions for medicines subject to special prescription rules, including controlled psychotropic or narcotic substances.

**Official source:** [Ministry of Health — Cross-border prescriptions](https://www.salute.gov.it/new/it/tema/cure-nellunione-europea/prescrizioni-farmacologiche/)

## Driving after cannabis use

Italy changed Article 187 of the Highway Code with Law no. 177/2024. The wording now penalizes driving **after taking narcotic or psychotropic substances**, and the provision includes criminal penalties and driving-licence consequences.

In January 2026, the Constitutional Court rejected constitutional challenges **but interpreted the provision in a way that prevents it from becoming an unlimited punishment for any remote past drug use**. The Court stressed the need for a meaningful temporal connection between drug use and driving, consistent with road-safety purposes.

For visitors and residents, the practical advice remains straightforward: **do not drive after cannabis use, and do not rely on a personal estimate of when traces or effects might cease to be legally relevant.**

**Official sources:**

- [Law 177/2024, Article 187 text — Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/2024/11/29/280/sg/pdf)
- [Constitutional Court judgment no. 10/2026 — Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/vediMenuHTML?atto.codiceRedazionale=T-260010&atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2026-02-04&tipoSerie=corte_costituzionale&tipoVigenza=originario)

## Can tourists buy or consume cannabis in Italy?

There is no legal recreational-cannabis market comparable to the regulated adult-use systems found in some other jurisdictions. A shop display, a familiar brand name or a low-THC claim should not be treated as proof that possession or consumption is lawful.

If you are visiting Italy:

- do not carry recreational cannabis across the border;
- do not assume “cannabis light” is a legally safe substitute after the 2025 changes;
- keep documentation for legitimately prescribed medicines and check the applicable controlled-medicine rules before travelling;
- never drive after cannabis use;
- for a specific product, seizure, charge or travel situation, obtain advice from a qualified Italian lawyer rather than relying on a general web guide.

## FAQ

### Is weed legal in Italy in 2026?

**No, recreational cannabis is not fully legal.** Personal-use possession may be handled under administrative rather than trafficking provisions when the legal requirements are met, but possession remains illicit and may lead to sanctions.

### Is cannabis light legal in Italy in 2026?

The answer is no longer captured by the old formula “low THC = legal.” Law 80/2025 introduced broad restrictions involving hemp inflorescences and products derived from them. Constitutional challenges are pending, so the legal picture should be checked against current official sources rather than old shop guidance.

### Is CBD legal in Italy?

CBD is not a single legal product category. Legality depends on the product, its composition and origin, its intended use and the sector-specific rules that apply. Claims such as “THC-free therefore legal” are too simplistic for the current Italian framework.

### Can I get medical cannabis in Italy?

Italy has a regulated medical-cannabis system based on medical prescription and pharmacy preparation/distribution. Access, reimbursement and product availability can vary, and foreign prescriptions for controlled medicines require particular caution.

### Can I drive after using cannabis?

Do not do so. Article 187 of the Highway Code regulates driving after drug use, and the Constitutional Court clarified the provision in 2026. The consequences can include criminal penalties and driving-licence sanctions.
