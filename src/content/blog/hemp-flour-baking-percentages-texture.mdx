## When a flour changes the dough

Adding hemp flour to bread or other baked goods sounds straightforward: replace part of the wheat flour and follow the usual recipe. But a flour blend does not necessarily behave like the original dough. Crumb colour, flavour, structure and the ability to retain gas during fermentation can all change.

A more useful question than “What is the perfect percentage?” is “What changes between blends, and how can I observe it?”. Bread studies provide valuable clues, but home results also depend on the flour, process and recipe.

## Why one flour cannot simply replace another

Wheat gluten-forming proteins are crucial to the elasticity of conventional leavened dough. Hemp seed flour does not perform the same function. Substituting wheat alters the proportion of gluten-forming ingredients while changing the blend’s fibre, lipid, non-gluten protein and particle-size profile.

Not all hemp flours are equivalent. Some are milled from the residue left after pressing hemp seeds for oil; others differ in fat content or fineness. Two products used at the same percentage can therefore yield different results. Water absorption cannot be reduced to a rule that applies to every hemp flour.

## What studies show at 10%, 15% and 25%

Researchers at the University of Parma examined wheat bread made with 10%, 15% and 25% hemp flour substitution, measuring loaf volume, crumb texture and changes during seven days of storage. In that specific formulation, the wheat control had a specific volume of about 3.4 L/kg, compared with 4.1 L/kg at 10%, 3.8 L/kg at 15% and 3.1 L/kg at 25% hemp flour. The 10% and 15% breads also had a softer crumb than the control. These measurements describe the study conditions, not a universal outcome for every flour or recipe. The authors considered 15% a promising technological compromise among the levels tested—not a universal rule for all recipes.

A separate sensory study using 10%, 15% and 25% hemp flour reported that acceptance of the 10% and 15% breads could remain comparable to the reference bread under its test conditions. Sensory results are formulation-specific and do not predict everyone’s preferences.

### A simple calculation for 500 g of total flour

For a flour blend weighing 500 g before water, salt and yeast are added:

0% hemp: 500 g wheat flour + 0 g hemp flour — reference batch.

10% hemp: 450 g wheat flour + 50 g hemp flour.

15% hemp: 425 g wheat flour + 75 g hemp flour.

25% hemp: 375 g wheat flour + 125 g hemp flour.

These are mathematical examples, not four tested recipes. The 25% level is particularly useful for understanding how a higher substitution changes performance; it is not a recommended starting point for every kitchen.

## A controlled home comparison

To see the effect of one ingredient, make two small batches: your usual recipe as a control, and the same formula with 10% of the total flour replaced by food-grade hemp flour. Keep wheat flour type, yeast, salt, mixing method, temperature and timing consistent. Avoid changing the oil, sugar and fermentation at the same time, or it will be harder to identify the reason for any differences.

Start with the hydration called for in your original recipe. Assess dough consistency as you mix, adding small measured amounts of water only if needed. There is no single scientifically established extra-water allowance for every hemp flour. Compare handling, shape retention, oven spring, crumb colour, aroma and texture. This is a suggested observation exercise, not a recipe tested by the editorial team.

### What to record

- Handling: does the dough stretch readily or tear?

- Rise: how does the final loaf volume compare with the control?

- Crumb: are the pores even, and is the texture denser or softer?

- Flavour: do you notice more pronounced earthy or toasted notes?

- Storage: how does crumb texture change over time? Follow appropriate food-storage practices; a texture study does not establish microbiological shelf life.

## Five common mistakes

### 1. Replacing all wheat flour with hemp flour

In conventional yeast bread, hemp flour cannot be treated as a one-for-one replacement for wheat. Gluten-free baking requires its own carefully developed formulas; simply removing wheat flour is not enough.

### 2. Treating a bread percentage as a universal baking rule

The amount tested in bread is not automatically ideal for pizza, focaccia, muffins or cookies. Product structure matters: research on muffins found different substitution outcomes for white wheat and whole-wheat bases. Start with the function required by the specific dough.

### 3. Assuming every hemp flour needs the same hydration adjustment

Fibre, proteins, fat content and particle size all shape interactions with water. Research findings do not point to a single direction for every ingredient. Measure and record changes instead of relying on a universal hydration shortcut.

### 4. Confusing hemp flour with protein concentrate

Ground hemp seed products, pressed seed cake flour and isolated or concentrated hemp proteins have different compositions. The results of studies on added hemp protein powders should not automatically be transferred to ordinary hemp flour.

### 5. Turning nutrition into health promises

Changes to fibre or protein content depend on the actual ingredients and final formulation. They do not prove medical benefits. Nutrition statements require product-specific data and compliance with applicable rules on public claims.

## Bread, pizza and cookies call for different tests

In bread, volume and crumb structure are central. Pizza dough places particular demands on stretchability and handling, while cookies and shortcrust pastries are often judged more by crumbliness, colour and flavour. Results from one bread study cannot be mechanically transferred to these other products.

For an initial experiment, choose a product you know well and alter one variable at a time. The goal is not to guarantee a “better” result, but to understand the balance between flavour, texture and ease of handling.

## Choosing and storing hemp flour

Check that the flour is explicitly sold for food use. Read the ingredient list, manufacturer’s instructions and allergen or cross-contact information. Hemp seed flour itself may be naturally gluten-free, but a recipe containing wheat flour contains gluten. People with medical dietary requirements must rely on appropriate ingredient and production assurances.

Follow the product label for storage, protecting it from moisture and heat as recommended. Products with different residual fat contents may have different storage requirements.

## The takeaway

Hemp flour can change a dough in interesting ways, but a percentage alone does not predict the result. Some bread experiments found favourable properties at 10–15% in specific formulations, while higher additions or different flours can behave differently. The most useful kitchen approach is to compare small measurable batches and judge the intended texture and flavour without mistaking a laboratory result for a universal recipe.
