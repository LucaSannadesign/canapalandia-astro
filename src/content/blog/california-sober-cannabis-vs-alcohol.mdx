<p>In a world where alcohol is celebrated in the golden halls of international expos and light cannabis is still demonized, some have chosen a different path: the path of awareness.</p>

<p>The <strong>California Sober</strong> movement, born in the United States and now spreading globally, offers a radically different approach: <strong>no alcohol, no hard drugs, but an openness to moderate and informed cannabis use</strong>.</p>

<p>A paradox? Only for those still trapped in the old prohibitionist slogans.</p>

  <hr />

  <h2>The choice to say no to alcohol (and yes to cannabis)</h2>

<p>Those embracing the California Sober path aren’t nostalgic hippies or anti-alcohol fanatics, but rather <strong>young people, artists, athletes, and adults seeking balance</strong>.</p>

<p>People who have realized that alcohol — although socially accepted — is a toxic substance, <strong>linked to cancer, car accidents, domestic violence, and premature death</strong>, especially among the young.</p>

<p>Cannabis, on the other hand, <strong>has never caused a documented death</strong>. It is increasingly studied for its therapeutic potential and, in many forms such as CBD or light cannabis, <strong>does not have debilitating psychoactive effects</strong>.</p>

  <hr />

  <h2>Cannabis and wellness: what research says</h2>

<p>Recent studies show that responsible cannabis use may help with:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>insomnia</li>
    <li>anxiety</li>
    <li>chronic stress</li>
    <li>dependence on more harmful substances (including alcohol)</li>
  </ul>

<p>Many former drinkers say they’ve found in cannabis a <strong>healthier refuge</strong>, one that doesn’t disconnect them from reality but helps them live it with greater clarity.</p>

  <hr />

  <h2>A movement with many shades</h2>

<p>As reported by the <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/wellness/2024/apr/11/california-sober-meaning-marijuana" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Guardian</a>, the concept of California Sober is not monolithic.  
  For some, it simply means avoiding alcohol and hard drugs; for others, it may include responsible use of cannabis and even mild psychedelics in controlled contexts.</p>

<p>What unites those who choose this path is the desire to <strong>embrace a more flexible, personal form of sobriety centered on real well-being</strong>, free from dogmas and cultural impositions.</p>

<p>It’s not about promoting indiscriminate use of cannabis, but about <strong>reassessing the role of legal and illegal substances in our society</strong>, putting people at the center — not prejudice.</p>

  <hr />

  <h2>And in Italy? A toast to misinformation</h2>

<p>While in the U.S. there&#8217;s talk of <em>“mindful cannabis”</em> as part of a healthy lifestyle, in Italy everything is still thrown into the fear-mongering pot.</p>

<p>One example? Agriculture Minister Francesco Lollobrigida, who in spring 2024 enthusiastically attended Vinitaly to <strong>promote Italian wine as a national excellence</strong>. Nothing wrong with that, except that the same government took a hard line against <strong>light cannabis</strong>, culminating in <strong>September 2024</strong> with the approval of an amendment to the <strong>Security Decree</strong> banning its production and sale — a ban that officially took effect in 2025.</p>

<p>Too bad no one at Vinitaly mentioned that alcohol is classified by the IARC as a <strong>Group 1 carcinogen</strong>, alongside asbestos and benzene.</p>

<p>Too bad that <strong>alcohol-related deaths exceed 30,000 per year in Italy alone</strong>.</p>

<p>And too bad that true light cannabis <strong>has never killed anyone</strong>.</p>

  <hr />

  <h2>California Sober is a healthy provocation</h2>

<p>You don’t have to live in California to embrace this lifestyle.  
  What’s needed is to <strong>free ourselves from the toxic narrative</strong> that wants us drunk and unaware, yet terrified of a flower.</p>
