> **In brief.** Terpenes are natural compounds in the broad isoprenoid family. In *Cannabis sativa*, the volatile fraction — especially monoterpenes, sesquiterpenes and related terpenoids — contributes to the plant’s smell. The profile changes with cultivar, tissue, developmental stage, growing environment and drying. On its own, however, it cannot reliably predict the overall aroma or a health effect.

When people talk about hemp and cannabis, terpenes are often presented as a kind of aromatic identity card for the plant. There is some truth in that idea, but it becomes misleading when a chemical profile is turned directly into promises about mood, sleep, pain or other health effects.

Not all terpenes are volatile. In *Cannabis sativa*, it is mainly monoterpenes, sesquiterpenes and related terpenoids in the volatile fraction that contribute to the odour profile together with other families of molecules. Recent research shows that this composition is dynamic: it changes between cultivars, tissues and developmental stages, and it cannot be reduced to a simple list of names printed on a label.

Understanding what terpene profiles actually measure helps separate chemistry, sensory perception and marketing. One point needs to be clear from the outset: much of the literature cited here studies *Cannabis sativa* with different cannabinoid profiles, including high-THC chemotypes, and not exclusively industrial hemp as defined by law. Chemical and pharmacological findings should therefore not be transferred automatically to every hemp product.

## What are terpenes?

In the strict chemical sense, according to IUPAC nomenclature, terpenes are biologically derived hydrocarbons whose carbon skeletons are formally derived from isoprene; terpenoids include related natural products containing oxygen in different functional groups. In biological and commercial language, the two terms are often used more broadly.

In plants, these molecules perform different ecological functions, from chemical communication to defence, and part of the volatile fraction contributes to the smell of leaves, flowers, resins and other plant tissues.

In everyday language and commercial analyses, “terpenes” is often used as an umbrella term that also includes related terpenoid compounds. For a non-specialist reader, the essential point is this: they are a broad group of molecules with different properties, not a single ingredient and not a category with one uniform effect.
