## Terpenes, cannabinoids and industrial hemp: do not mix up the categories

Terpenes are not cannabinoids. THC and CBD belong to a different chemical class and have specific pharmacological properties. A sample can have a certain terpene profile without that, by itself, saying how much THC or CBD it contains.

In addition, the literature on *Cannabis sativa* often includes different chemotypes and cultivars, not only industrial hemp in the Italian legal sense. Findings on aroma and the volatilome should therefore be read as botanical and chemical data, not as a legal classification of the product.

Likewise, an aromatic profile does not determine the legality of a product or cultivation. For the Italian regulatory framework, the applicable rules and the intended use of the product matter, not its smell or the presence of a particular terpene. For a general overview, see [Cannabis light and CBD in Italy: what the law says in 2026](/blog/cannabis-light-cbd-legale-italia-2026/).

## In summary

Terpenes are a real and measurable part of *Cannabis sativa* chemistry, but interpreting them requires more precision than many commercial charts suggest.

Composition varies with genetics, tissue, development, growing environment and post-harvest management. The terpene profile does not by itself describe the full perceived aroma and, above all, it does not automatically demonstrate a clinical effect. Research on terpene-cannabinoid interactions has produced both positive and negative findings, including *in vitro* data from 2026 and small human studies from 2024–2025. The so-called entourage effect therefore cannot be treated as a predictable therapeutic property based on a list of terpenes.

For readers, the most useful distinction is simple: the terpene profile is chemical data; aroma is a complex sensory phenomenon; a health effect requires specific clinical evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are terpenes the same thing as CBD or THC?

No. Terpenes belong to a different chemical family from cannabinoids. A terpene profile does not automatically indicate the concentration of CBD or THC.

### Can a terpene tell you whether a product is relaxing or stimulating?

Not reliably. Studies on isolated molecules may suggest biological activity, but the presence of a terpene is not enough to predict a clinical effect in a person.

### Does the terpene profile determine aroma?

It contributes to aroma, but it does not determine it on its own. Recent sensory studies show that other volatile compounds and their interactions matter, and that terpene profiles can be poor predictors of overall perception.
